How can I Make Friends (Even If You Feel Awkward or Shy)

Making friends seems like something that should come naturally. But at some point in life, it stops being easy.

calendar_today April 14, 2026 schedule 5 min read person CareActs Team

Making friends seems like something that should come naturally.

But at some point in life, it stops being easy.

Maybe you’ve noticed it too—how effortless it used to feel when you were younger. You’d sit next to someone, share a few laughs, and somehow, a friendship would just… happen.

Now it’s different.

People are busier. Conversations feel more forced. And sometimes, even when you want to connect with someone, you don’t know how to start.

If you’ve been feeling this way, you’re not alone. And more importantly—you’re not stuck like this.

Learning how to make friends isn’t about changing who you are. It’s about understanding how connection actually works.

It’s Not That You Can’t Make Friends—You’re Just Out of Practice

Most people think something is wrong with them.

“I’m too awkward.”
“I don’t know what to say.”
“People probably won’t like me.”

But the truth is much simpler.

You haven’t lost the ability to make friends—you’ve just had fewer opportunities to practice it.

As adults, we don’t get built-in social environments like school anymore. We have to create those opportunities ourselves. And that’s where most people struggle. Not because they’re incapable—but because they’re not showing up.

Friendships Don’t Start Big—They Start Small

One of the biggest mistakes people make is expecting instant connection.

They think friendship starts with deep conversations, emotional bonding, or instant chemistry.

It doesn’t.

It starts with something much smaller.

  • A simple “hi.”
  • A short conversation.
  • A shared moment that lasts just a few minutes.

That’s it. These small interactions might feel insignificant, but they’re actually the foundation of every strong friendship. Without them, nothing grows.

You Have to Put Yourself Where Connection Can Happen

This is the part most people avoid—but it’s also the most important.

You can’t make friends if you’re always alone.

It sounds obvious, but many people expect friendships to appear without changing their environment. They stay in the same routine, meet the same people, and wonder why nothing changes.

If you want new friends, you need new spaces.

It could be something simple: joining a class, going to a gym, attending events, or even participating in online communities that lead to real conversations.

The goal isn’t to be perfect socially. The goal is to be present where interaction is possible. Because friendship needs opportunity.

Stop Trying to Be Interesting—Start Being Interested

There’s a lot of pressure to say the “right” thing.

To sound smart. Funny. Impressive.

But that pressure is exactly what makes conversations feel awkward.

You don’t need to be interesting. You need to be interested.

Ask simple questions. Listen carefully. Respond naturally. When people feel heard, they relax. And when they relax, connection becomes easier.

It Will Feel Uncomfortable—and That’s Normal

No one talks about this enough.

Making friends can feel awkward.

Starting conversations feels risky. Asking someone to hang out feels even riskier. You might hesitate. You might overthink. You might even avoid it completely.

But that discomfort isn’t a sign you’re doing something wrong. It’s a sign you’re stepping outside your routine. And that’s exactly where new connections begin.

Not Everyone Will Be Your Person

This is something you need to accept early.

You won’t connect with everyone—and not everyone will connect with you. Sometimes conversations won’t flow. Sometimes people won’t respond the way you hoped.

That’s normal.

Friendship isn’t about being liked by everyone. It’s about finding the few people where things feel natural. So instead of taking it personally, just move forward. The right connections don’t feel forced.

Real Friendships Are Built Over Time

A single conversation doesn’t create a friendship.

What does?

Seeing someone again. Talking again. Remembering small details. Slowly becoming familiar with each other.

This is the part many people skip. They expect instant results and give up too quickly. But real friendships grow slowly—and that’s what makes them strong.

Final Thoughts

If you’ve been wondering how to make friends, here’s the truth: It’s not about being perfect, confident, or naturally social. It’s about showing up. Starting small. Staying consistent. And allowing connections to grow over time.

You don’t need a large circle. Just a few genuine friendships can completely change how you feel in your daily life.

So don’t wait for the “perfect moment” or the “perfect words.” Start with something simple.

A hello. A smile. A small conversation.

That’s where every friendship begins.
